# On-Premises vs Cloud Computing
## Overview

This document compares On-Premises (On-Prem) and Cloud Computing deployment models. It outlines key differences in ownership, cost, scalability, and management to help organizations choose the most suitable infrastructure strategy.

## On-Premises (On-Prem)
### Definition

In an On-Premises environment, all hardware, software, and data are hosted and managed locally within an organization’s own facilities. The organization is responsible for purchasing, installing, and maintaining servers, storage, and network components.

### Advantages

* Full Control: Complete ownership and management of infrastructure and data.

* Enhanced Security: Data remains within the organization’s physical boundaries, which can simplify compliance with certain regulations.

* Customization: Systems and applications can be fully customized to specific business or technical needs.

* Consistent Performance: Performance is not affected by internet connectivity or external service providers.

### Disadvantages

* High Upfront Cost: Requires significant capital investment in hardware, software, and physical space.

* Ongoing Maintenance: In-house IT teams must manage updates, patches, and hardware failures.

* Limited Scalability: Scaling up typically requires purchasing and installing new equipment.

* Disaster Recovery Complexity: Backup and recovery solutions must be implemented and managed internally.
## Cloud Computing
### Definition

Cloud Computing delivers computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, and software—over the internet. Cloud resources are provided and managed by third-party providers such as AWS, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud, Oracle.

### Advantages

* Scalability: Easily scale resources up or down based on business needs.

* Cost Efficiency: Pay only for the resources you use, reducing upfront expenses.

* Reduced Maintenance: Cloud providers handle system updates, patches, and infrastructure management.

* Global Accessibility: Users can access systems and data from any location with internet access.

* Built-in Resilience: Cloud platforms offer redundancy, failover, and disaster recovery capabilities by default.

### Disadvantages

* Limited Control: Infrastructure and data are hosted by a third party, reducing direct control.

* Ongoing Costs: Continuous operational expenses may accumulate over time.

* Internet Dependency: Requires stable internet connectivity for optimal performance.

* Compliance Considerations: Organizations must ensure providers comply with applicable data protection and privacy regulations.
